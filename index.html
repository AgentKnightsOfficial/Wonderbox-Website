Why we started WonderBox

It began in hospital hallways, with a group of friends who kept noticing the same thing.

We are a group of high school students. During the school year and through our summers, we volunteered in hospitals.

What stayed with us was how hard these stays can be on children. They are away from home, their friends, and everything familiar, often for days or weeks at a time. Medicine takes care of the illness. Nobody has much time to take care of the child's spirits.

So we decided to do something about it together. We started WonderBox to give hospitalized kids something that is just for them. It is a care package built for their age, delivered free, and a reminder that people they have never met are thinking of them.

Ways to help

Three boxes for three ages

A four-year-old and a sixteen-year-old do not want the same thing. Pick an age group to see what goes inside.

Toddlers
Ages 2 to 5

Soft, bright, and easy to hold. Comfort comes first for the youngest patients.

  • Mini Play-Doh
  • 4-pack of crayons with coloring sheets
  • Board book or sticker book
  • Chunky puzzle or stacking cups
  • Bath toy or soft block
School-age
Ages 6 to 12

Things to build, draw, and puzzle over, so long afternoons have something to do.

  • 4-pack of crayons or colored pencils with coloring sheets
  • Word search sheet
  • A pencil
  • Small LEGO-style set or card game
  • Fidget spinner, stress ball, or worry stone
Teens
Ages 13 to 17

Made for teens, not scaled up from a little kid's box. Things that feel like they belong to them.

  • Puzzle book
  • Pencil or pen with a small journal
  • Fidget spinner, stress ball, or worry stone
  • Deck of cards
  • Adult coloring book or small craft kit

Exact contents change with what donors and partners provide.

How a WonderBox reaches a child

  1. We gather

    We raise money and collect donated toys, games, books, and comfort items.

  2. We pack

    Each box is sorted and packed for one age group, then checked before it goes out.

  3. We deliver

    Boxes are made for pediatric patients at Connecticut Children's in Hartford and Yale New Haven Children's Hospital, at no cost to their families.

  4. A child opens it

    Someone who has spent days indoors gets something that is just for them.
